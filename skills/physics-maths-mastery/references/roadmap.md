## Month-by-month (Checkpoint 1)
- **Month 1 (wk 1-4)**: diagnostic (Khan Academy unit tests), set up Anki, notes, Python (CS50P). Calculus completion (3Blue1Brown Essence of Calculus, MIT 18.01SC), vectors, Newtonian mechanics (MIT 8.01 Lewin, OpenStax University Physics Vol. 1), multivariable begins. Gate 1.
- **Month 2 (wk 5-8)**: finish MIT 18.02SC; linear algebra in full (3Blue1Brown Essence of Linear Algebra, MIT 18.06 Strang; eigenvalues, Hermitian matrices, diagonalisation, complex vector spaces); oscillations and waves (MIT 8.03 selected); complex numbers begin. Gate 2.
- **Month 3 (wk 9-12)**: ODEs (MIT 18.03SC, 3Blue1Brown differential equations); finish complex numbers (MIT 18.04 early lectures or Beck et al. free text); start Fourier series; electromagnetism (MIT 8.02 Lewin, Feynman Lectures Vol. II online, Tong's Electromagnetism as stretch) through Maxwell's equations and EM waves. Gate 3.
- **Month 4 (wk 13-16)**: finish Fourier and PDEs (Steve Brunton, late 18.03SC); probability (Khan, MIT 18.05 selected); thermal and statistical physics to the Boltzmann distribution, equipartition and the black-body problem (Susskind Statistical Mechanics, Tong, Feynman Vol. I); begin Lagrangian mechanics (Susskind Classical Mechanics, Tong Classical Dynamics). Gate 4. Friday history reading starts.
- **Month 5 (wk 17-20)**: finish Hamiltonian mechanics (Noether, Poisson brackets); special relativity (Susskind SR and Classical Field Theory, Feynman Vol. I, MIT 8.033 problems, Einstein's Relativity on Project Gutenberg); old quantum theory (Planck, photoelectric effect, Compton, Bohr, de Broglie, Davisson-Germer, Stern-Gerlach; MIT 8.04 first lectures, Feynman Vol. III); special functions just in time; Schrodinger equation begins. Gate 5.
- **Month 6 (wk 21-24)**: quantum mechanics completed (wells, tunnelling, oscillator, hydrogen, Hilbert space and Dirac notation, matrix mechanics, commutators and uncertainty, angular momentum and spin, identical particles and exclusion, basic perturbation theory, Dirac equation survey; Susskind QM, MIT 8.04 and selected 8.05, Tong QM, Feynman Vol. III); tensor calculus begins (eigenchris). Gate 6.
- **Month 7 (wk 25-28)**: finish tensors (metric, covariant derivative, Christoffel symbols, geodesics, curvature; Carroll's free GR notes, Schuller stretch); general relativity (equivalence principle, field equations, Schwarzschild, perihelion of Mercury, 1919 light bending, redshift, weak-field gravitational waves, Friedmann 1922, Lemaitre 1927, Hubble 1929; Susskind GR and Cosmology, Tong GR, MIT 8.286 Guth). Gate 7.
- **Weeks 29-30**: capstone (pick two: Schwarzschild geodesic simulation in Python; numerical 1D Schrodinger solver compared with analytic results; a 3,000-word "1900-1930 story explained to my past self" plus a 20-minute talk), buffer, Checkpoint 1 review.

## Checkpoint 1 competency checklist (use for gates)
- Mechanics: double pendulum equations of motion from a Lagrangian; Hamilton's equations; energy conservation from Noether's theorem.
- Electromagnetism: Gauss and Ampere on symmetric setups; wave equation and the speed of light from Maxwell's equations.
- Thermal physics: Boltzmann distribution; equipartition; why classical physics fails for black-body radiation.
- Special relativity: time dilation, length contraction, velocity addition; four-vectors in collisions.
- Quantum mechanics: infinite well, oscillator, hydrogen levels; commutators; spin-1/2 measurement sequences; Born rule and uncertainty.
- General relativity: Christoffel symbols for a simple metric; geodesic equation; light bending and perihelion precession in Schwarzschild; the Friedmann equations.
- History: Planck 1900, Einstein 1905, Bohr 1913, general relativity 1915, Eddington's eclipse 1919, de Broglie 1924, Heisenberg and Pauli 1925, Schrodinger and Born 1926, uncertainty and the Solvay conference 1927, Hubble 1929.

## Checkpoints 2-5 (estimates; re-plan at each review)
- **Checkpoint 2**: maths (about 550 h): real analysis (MIT 18.100, Trench free text), linear algebra at proof level (Hefferon free text), abstract algebra (Macauley's lectures, MIT 18.701), point-set topology (MIT 18.901), complex analysis in full (MIT 18.04), manifolds and forms (Schuller), measure and probability (Tao's 245A notes). Physics (about 450 h): graduate QM (MIT 8.05 and 8.06, Tong Topics in QM and Applications of QM), electrodynamics (MIT 8.07, Tong), statistical physics (MIT 8.044, Kardar MIT 8.333), classical field theory and relativistic wave equations. Gate: prove Cauchy's theorem, Lagrange's theorem, Heine-Borel; graduate problem sets at 70%.
- **Checkpoint 3**: QFT (Tong's notes, Coleman's Harvard lectures with arXiv notes, Srednicki's free draft), Standard Model and gauge theory (Tong), Lie groups and representations; GR and cosmology (Carroll, Reall's black-hole notes, Baumann and Tong cosmology); functional analysis (MIT 18.102), fibre bundles (Schuller), algebraic topology begins (Hatcher, free). Gate: tree-level amplitudes, Higgs mechanism, Schwarzschild and FRW results, cosmological perturbations outline.
- **Checkpoint 4**: choose a branch or alternate. Physics: supersymmetry (Martin's primer), CFT (Ginsparg), string theory (Tong, Zwiebach MIT 8.251, then Polchinski or Becker-Becker-Schwarz), AdS/CFT (McGreevy, Maldacena notes), black-hole information (Harlow), loop quantum gravity for contrast (Rovelli). Maths by problem: P vs NP (MIT 18.404 and 6.045, Arora-Barak draft, Aaronson's survey; most accessible, can start as a side thread in Checkpoint 2); Riemann Hypothesis (complex analysis then analytic number theory notes, Tao, Conrad; Bombieri's Clay description); Yang-Mills mass gap (Checkpoint 3 then constructive QFT; Jaffe-Witten); Navier-Stokes (MIT 18.152, Tong fluids, Tao; Fefferman); Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer (MIT 18.783, Milne; Wiles); Hodge conjecture (Vakil's Rising Sea, Hatcher; Deligne). Theory of everything and quantum gravity are research programmes, not single problems.
- **Checkpoint 5**: narrow to a specific question, read arXiv regularly, write expository notes, join communities (MathOverflow, Physics Stack Exchange, recorded seminars), contact researchers; a graduate programme is the usual route to original results.

Free-resource availability changes. Verify before relying on a specific link, unit name or chapter number.
